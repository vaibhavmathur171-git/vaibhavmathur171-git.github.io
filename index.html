Magic Leap 2 · Spatial dimming

Making virtual content visible outdoors.

Why dimming matters

See-through AR glasses add an image to the light already coming from the world. In bright sunlight, that image can look faint or washed out. Spatial dimming reduces the light behind virtual content, improving contrast while leaving the rest of the view clear.

From research to a shipped device

I invented a new type of see-through LCD technology for spatial dimming and led its development from fundamental research through prototypes, manufacturing and a shipped feature in Magic Leap 2.

03 / MEMS & medical imaging2010–2016 · Axsun / Excelitas

A tiny mirror.
Faster imaging.

At Axsun, I developed MEMS tunable filters for swept-source OCT. Moving a microscopic mirror tunes the laser wavelength, enabling fast imaging of tissue beneath the surface.

I worked across the whole process: device design, CAD, finite element analysis in COMSOL, fabrication, characterization and manufacturing support.

04 / Where it beganPhD, Applied Physics · UMass Lowell · 2010

Light in.
Mirror motion out.

At UMass Lowell, I integrated MEMS mirrors with photodetectors so light could address their electrostatic motion—a route toward denser adaptive-optics arrays that correct distorted light.

I designed and fabricated the devices, then measured how they behaved against my models.

Holding particles
with light.

At the Indian Institute of Science, I got my first experience running an optical experiment, from designing the setup and aligning the optics to taking measurements.

I worked on optical tweezers—tightly focused light that holds microscopic particles—and coauthored research on measuring the trap’s response. These tools also enable experiments on DNA mechanics using trapped beads.

2015 → ongoing

What got me thinking about AI.

In 2015, I read Tim Urban’s Wait But Why series and Nick Bostrom’s Superintelligence. I’ve been following AI closely ever since. Their ideas about artificial general intelligence (AGI)—AI with broad human-level capabilities—and artificial superintelligence (ASI)—AI that goes beyond us across most cognitive tasks—stayed with me. Kurzweil and Diamandis gave me more to think about, and these questions still influence what I choose to build.
