MEMS · 2010–2016

A silicon machine that sweeps a laser 200,000 times a second

A MEMS mirror is a machine carved from silicon: a membrane on microscopic springs, moved by electrical forces and resonating 200,000 times a second. Inside medical scanners called OCT, it tunes a laser's color so doctors can see beneath the surface of tissue.

I owned it across every domain: the mechanics of the springs and resonance, the electrical drive, the optics, and fabrication on silicon wafers through testing and production.

Research · 2004–2010

Where it started: using light as a force

PhD, Applied Physics · 2010

Mirrors moved by light alone

An array of 900 tiny mirrors that move when light shines on them. Each pixel combines a photodiode, a thin-film resistor and a mirror plate on springs, so light in becomes motion out: optics, electronics and mechanics in one chip. I designed, fabricated and measured them.

500 nm of motion from just 5 mW of light

Applied Physics Letters · first author ↗
First research · 2004

Holding a bead with a laser

Optical tweezers: a focused laser holds a bead a hundredth the width of a hair, like a mass on a spring. I built the setup and measured how stiff that spring is.

Trap frequency measured to 0.1%, better than previous methods
